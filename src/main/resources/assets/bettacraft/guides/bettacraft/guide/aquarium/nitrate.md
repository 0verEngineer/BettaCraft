# Nitrate and water changes

The filters turn the waste of the animals into **nitrate**. It is harmless at first, but **only water changes and
plants remove it**: even a well filtered aquarium needs fresh water now and then. The tab **Water** of the tank menu
shows it (hover over the line for an explanation): mg/L, how it stands and how much it rises per day.

| Difficulty (`waterchange.difficulty`) | 25 % fresh water about every |
|---|---|
| OFF | never (no nitrate) |
| EASY | 20 days |
| **NORMAL** (default) | **14 days** |
| REALISTIC | 7 days |

These intervals keep a **normally stocked** aquarium (one adult fish per two water blocks) below 40 mg/L. More fish
make nitrate faster, fewer slower. **Plants** take it up: a densely planted aquarium in good light (or many floating
plants) needs few or no water changes.

**Too much nitrate:**

* from **50 mg/L**: the fish breed only half as eagerly, algae grow faster, the tank log warns;
* from **120 mg/L**: the water counts as bad: the animals get sick and in the end die (only after many weeks without
  any water change).

Evaporation concentrates the nitrate like the salt; topping off with fresh water dilutes it back (no water change).
A water change by bucket or with the [water change controller](../equipment/water_change.md) lowers it: 25 % fresh
water takes away a quarter. Aquariums from older versions start at 0.
