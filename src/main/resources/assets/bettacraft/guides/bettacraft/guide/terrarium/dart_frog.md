# Poison dart frogs

Three small frogs (4-5 cm) of the rainforest floor of northern South America, one entity with three species as varieties:
the **dyeing poison dart frog** (*Dendrobates tinctorius*, here its sky-blue "azureus" form with black spots), the
**green-and-black poison dart frog** (*D. auratus*) and the **yellow-banded poison dart frog** (*D. leucomelas*). Their
colours warn of the poison of wild frogs, which comes from the ants and mites they eat. **Frogs raised in captivity
are not poisonous**: their food has none of those toxins; the tank animals are harmless.

## Care

| | |
|---|---|
| Tank | a [paludarium](../terrarium.md) with mostly land (50-95 %) and a little water |
| Air | 21 to 28 °C; **humidity 75 to 100 %** ([mister](mister.md), plants on the land, a lid) |
| Water | 20 to 28 °C |
| Food | [fruit flies](feeder_insects.md) (they are too small for crickets) |
| Stocking | 0.5 bettas each; keep a small group |

* They hop over the land in short jumps, rest between them and sometimes swim a little.
* Too warm (above 28 °C) harms them as much as cold; a heat mat with a low target is enough.

## Breeding

A pair of the **same species** lays 3-6 eggs onto moss, leaves or soil on the land. In the wild the father carries the
tadpoles to water; in the tank the **tadpoles** hatch straight into the water. They swim, eat pellets or fry food and
become froglets at 40 % of their growth. See [amphibians and reptiles](herps.md).
